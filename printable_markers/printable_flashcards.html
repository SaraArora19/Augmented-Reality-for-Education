Human Anatomy

The Human Heart (Cardiovascular System)

  • Identify the four chambers (Right/Left Atrium and Ventricles).
  • Understand systemic vs. pulmonary blood circulation.
  • Explain the role of cardiac valves in ensuring unidirectional flow.
Key Mechanism: The left ventricle has a thick muscular myocardium required to pump oxygenated blood at high pressure into systemic circulation through the aorta.
Right Atrium: Receives deox blood
Right Ventricle: Pumps to lungs
Left Atrium: Receives oxy blood
Left Ventricle: Pumps to systemic aorta

Human Anatomy

The Human Brain (Central Nervous System)

  • Distinguish the cerebrum, cerebellum, and brainstem.
  • Identify functional specialization of frontal, parietal, temporal, and occipital lobes.
  • Understand involuntary autonomic regulation in the brainstem.
Key Mechanism: The brain consumes 20% of resting oxygen and glucose despite representing only 2% of total body mass. The cerebellum coordinates voluntary balance and motor timing.
Frontal Lobe: Executive function
Occipital Lobe: Visual processing
Cerebellum: Motor balance
Brainstem: Vitals & breathing

Mechanics & Physics

Four-Stroke IC Engine (Otto Cycle)

  • Identify the four strokes: Intake, Compression, Power, and Exhaust.
  • Observe piston reciprocation translating into crankshaft rotational torque.
  • Understand that 2 complete crankshaft turns (720°) produce 1 power stroke.
Key Mechanism: The flywheel preserves angular momentum across idle strokes (intake, compression, exhaust) to ensure steady rotational speed.
Piston: Thermal gas expansion
Connecting Rod: Linear-rotary link
Crankshaft: Rotary torque output
Spark Plug: Air-fuel ignition

Mechanics & Physics

DC Electric Motor (Electromagnetism)

  • Demonstrate Lorentz force on current-carrying wire: F = I(L x B).
  • Analyze how the split-ring commutator reverses current to sustain continuous rotation.
  • Apply Fleming's Left-Hand Rule for magnetic thrust direction.
Key Mechanism: Without the split-ring commutator, the rotating coil would stall vertically at equilibrium. The commutator reverses coil polarity every 180°.
Permanent Stator: Magnetic B-field
Armature Coil: Current conductor
Commutator: Polarity reverser
Carbon Brushes: Sliding power feed

Plant Biology

Plant Cell Ultrastructure

  • Distinguish cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts, and large central vacuole.
  • Understand hydrostatic turgor pressure and structural rigidity.
  • Examine intracellular organelle compartmentalization.
Key Mechanism: The central vacuole stores water and applies outward pressure against the rigid cell wall. Loss of water drops turgor pressure, leading to wilting.
Cell Wall: Cellulose tensile support
Chloroplast: Photosynthesis site
Central Vacuole: Turgor regulator
Nucleus: Genetic directive

3D Geometry

Platonic Solids & Spatial Polyhedra

  • Verify Euler's characteristic formula V - E + F = 2.
  • Explore the 5 regular convex polyhedra in 3D Euclidean space.
  • Inspect vertices, edges, face symmetry, and dual polyhedral pairings.
Key Mechanism: For an icosahedron: 12 Vertices - 30 Edges + 20 Faces = 2. The regular octahedron is the topological geometric dual of the cube.
Vertices (V): Coordinate intersections
Edges (E): Linear polygon boundaries
Faces (F): Enclosing 2D regular planes
Euler Invariant: V - E + F = 2

Chemistry

DNA Double Helix (Macromolecular Structure)

  • Identify Watson-Crick base pairing: Adenine-Thymine (2 H-bonds) & Guanine-Cytosine (3 H-bonds).
  • Understand antiparallel 5' to 3' strand orientation.
  • Observe major and minor grooves crucial for protein binding.
Key Mechanism: Guanine-Cytosine base pairs possess three hydrogen bonds compared to Adenine-Thymine's two, yielding higher thermal denaturation resistance.
Backbone: Sugar-phosphate diester
A-T Pair: 2 Hydrogen bonds
G-C Pair: 3 Hydrogen bonds
Pitch: 10.5 base pairs / turn

Chemistry

Water Molecule (H2O Polarity & VSEPR)

  • Explain the bent molecular geometry (104.5° bond angle).
  • Analyze sp3 orbital hybridization with two non-bonding lone pairs on oxygen.
  • Explore dipole polarity and intermolecular hydrogen bonding.
Key Mechanism: Strong lone pair-lone pair electrostatic repulsion compresses the ideal 109.5° tetrahedral angle to 104.5°, creating a powerful molecular dipole moment.
Central Oxygen: Electronegative hub
Hydrogens: Partial positive charges
Bond Angle: 104.5 degrees
Network: Hydrogen bonding lattice
